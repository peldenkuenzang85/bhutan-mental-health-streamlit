# Student Wellbeing Prediction — Questions and Answers

## 1. Why was Logistic Regression selected?

**Answer:** Logistic Regression was selected because it is simple, easy to understand, and suitable for predicting two outcomes: **support needed or not needed**.

## 2. Why is the dataset divided into training and testing sets?

**Answer:** The training data is used to teach the model, while the testing data is used to check how well the model works on unseen students.

## 3. What does one-hot encoding do?

**Answer:** It converts categorical information, such as **gender or district**, into numerical values that the model can understand.

## 4. Why are numeric variables standardized?

**Answer:** Standardization puts numeric variables on a similar scale, so variables with larger values do not have an unnecessary influence on the model.

## 5. What is the difference between accuracy and recall?

**Answer:** **Accuracy** shows how many predictions are correct overall. **Recall** shows how well the model identifies students who actually need support.

## 6. What does ROC-AUC measure?

**Answer:** ROC-AUC measures how well the model can distinguish between students who **need support and do not need support**.

## 7. Why can this application not diagnose mental illness?

**Answer:** The application only predicts support need from the available data. It is not a medical or psychological assessment and cannot replace a qualified professional.

## 8. What are the advantages and limitations of synthetic data?

**Answer:** Synthetic data is useful because it is **safe, private, and easy to generate**. However, it may not fully represent the real experiences and conditions of Bhutanese students.

## 9. What ethical approval would real data collection require?

**Answer:** Real student data collection would require **ethical approval, informed consent, privacy protection, and appropriate permission from the relevant institution or ethics committee**.

## 10. How could the model be improved in future research?

**Answer:** Future research could use **real and larger datasets, more relevant features, other machine-learning models, better validation, and collaboration with mental-health professionals**.
